Why Wildfires Are Destroying More Buildings: Assessing the Past Two Decades of Building Destruction in the United States
Amanda Carlson
Physical Research Scientist, USGS Geosciences and Environmental Change Science Center · Fort Collins, CO
🕐 12:00 – 1:00 PM 📍 112 Hilgard Hall
Bio & Abstract
Bio: Amanda Carlson is a Physical Research Scientist at the USGS Geosciences and Environmental Change Science Center, with a PhD in Ecology from Colorado State University and undergraduate degrees in Environmental Science and Earth and Planetary Science from UC Berkeley. Her work focuses on using geospatial data, remote sensing, and machine learning to understand patterns of wildfire risk and growth of the wildland-urban interface in the U.S.

Abstract: Highly destructive wildfires are occurring more frequently across the globe, prompting debates over the causes for this increase and effective management responses. We investigated building destruction trends in the United States by mapping buildings that were exposed to and destroyed by wildfires over two decades. The proportion of exposed buildings that were destroyed more than tripled from 2002–2022, indicating that wildfires became more destructive in addition to burning more populated areas. This increase in destruction rate was linked to greater building exposure in forests, but destruction rates also increased in grass and shrublands and were influenced by weather, development type, and burn severity. Our findings suggest that diverse management approaches may be needed to reduce community vulnerability to wildfire disasters.

Who Wins With Up-Zoning? Permitting Reforms for Accessory Dwelling Units
Philip Hoxie
PhD Candidate in Economics, UC San Diego · Research Affiliate, UCSD Center for Economic Policy Analysis
🕐 12:00 – 1:00 PM 📍 Bauer Wurster, Room 106
Abstract
Can up-zoning small units increase housing supply and who captures the benefits? This talk studies California's 2017 deregulation of Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) through state-preemption of local permitting, exploiting a regulatory discontinuity at the California Coastal Zone boundary. Results show that permitting reforms increased ADU permitting activity by about 30% and land values by 7%, with implications for housing equity and urban planning policy.

GeoAI for Aging Research: A New Agenda for Gerontological GIS
Prof. Kai Cao
Professor & Zijiang Outstanding Young Scholar, School of Geographic Sciences, East China Normal University
🕐 11:00 AM – 12:00 PM 📍 Bauer Wurster, Room 315A
Abstract
The global acceleration of population aging presents profound challenges and opportunities for interdisciplinary research. This talk introduces an emerging frontier — GeoAI for Aging Research — and articulates a new agenda for Gerontological GIS that moves beyond conventional mapping and description toward prediction, explanation, and optimization. Case studies span three core areas: identifying older adults' spatial behaviors using mobility data and deep learning; revealing environmental mechanisms affecting older adults' wellbeing; and optimizing age-friendly urban environments. Key challenges, research directions, and ethical considerations are also discussed.
